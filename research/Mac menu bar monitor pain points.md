# Fragile sensors and lying status sink rivals

The closest rivals to Sparewatt are **iStat Menus 7** (paid, the most complete, with a "Combined" single-item mode) and **Stats** (free, MIT, about 43k GitHub stars, one maintainer). Each overlaps with Sparewatt's battery, disk and file features only in part. Around them sit single-purpose tools: AlDente, Battery Toolkit and coconutBattery for charging; TG Pro, Macs Fan Control and Hot for temperatures; DaisyDisk, GrandPerspective and OmniDiskSweeper for folder sizes; Dropover and Yoink for file shelves; and Bartender and Ice for menu bar crowding. Their complaints fall into four themes that recur across categories. **First, sensors break with every new Apple silicon chip.** Stats' most-reacted issues are "No sensors" on each new generation. **Second, every macOS release breaks something.** macOS 27 broke nearly every third-party charge limiter and every menu bar manager in 2026. **Third, status displays mislead.** AlDente can look normal while the Mac is overcharging, and users cannot tell whether Apple's 80% limit means "bypass" or "cycling". **Fourth, the menu bar is crowded,** and managers only hide icons; they do not reduce them. Sparewatt is built to read data, not write it: it owns one status item and does not write to the SMC (the chip that controls charging and fans). That design already sidesteps most of this breakage. Its biggest open opportunities are a **mismatch alarm** (intended charge state versus measured current), **history and sustained-threshold alerts**, and a **disk reconciliation line** that explains "missing" space.

## Two incumbents split the market, and a long tail is stale

**iStat Menus 7.5** (Bjango, macOS 13.5+) covers CPU per core, GPU, memory, disks, network, sensors, battery, weather and clocks. It adds "Rules" alerts, history graphs, fan control, and a **Combined mode that merges items into one menu bar item**, which is the nearest match to Sparewatt's grouping ([Bjango](https://bjango.com/mac/istatmenus/)). It launched at **$11.99 new / $9.99 upgrade** ([Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2024/08/01/istat-menus-7)). It is also on Setapp, and weather becomes a separate subscription after six months ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6499559693)). Bjango justifies paid upgrades by the need to buy each new Mac to support its sensors ([Bjango help](https://bjango.com/help/istatmenus7/update/)). **Stats** covers CPU, GPU, RAM, disk, network, battery, sensors and Bluetooth for free. It has **one maintainer** and has relegated fan control to "not maintained" ([GitHub](https://github.com/exelban/stats)).

The tail is thinner. **eul** says its App Store build is outdated and that it removed all SMC calls, so it has no temperatures or fans ([GitHub](https://github.com/gao-sun/eul)), and AlternativeTo lists it as discontinued ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/eul/about/)). **MenuMeters** still releases but needed five point releases to reach usable Apple silicon temperature support ([releases](https://github.com/yujitach/MenuMeters/releases)). **iStatistica Pro** ($9.99) needs a separate sensors plugin ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1447778660)). **Sensei** costs $59 one-time or $29/yr and has a known license bug that shows "Expired" after renewal ([Cindori support](https://cindori.com/support/sensei/general/subscription-license-expired)). **Hot** can show only thermal pressure on Apple silicon ([GitHub](https://github.com/macmade/Hot)).

## Sensor and OS breakage is the category's chronic disease

The best-measured complaint is fragile data collection. Stats' most-reacted issues are **#210 "No sensors – Apple Silicon" (98 reactions)**, **#2249 "No sensors for M4" (61)** and **#1286, broken M2 values (32)** ([GitHub](https://github.com/exelban/stats/issues?q=is%3Aissue+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)). The maintainer explains that "Apple changes sensor keys with each new SoC" ([README](https://github.com/exelban/stats)). The currently most-discussed open issue is **#3621, "CPU Power Showing 0.00W"** ([GitHub](https://github.com/exelban/stats/issues?q=is%3Aissue+is%3Aopen+sort%3Acomments-desc)). That is the worst kind of failure: a plausible-looking number that is actually a bug. TG Pro documents the same churn: GPU sensors go silent at low load, and an M4 Max shows duplicate SSD sensors ([Tunabelly](https://www.tunabellysoftware.com/blog/files/tag-m4.html)).

OS releases add a second layer. On macOS 26.4, Stats items vanished because of Tahoe's new Menu Bar permission. The issue thread grew heated enough to be locked ([#3120](https://github.com/exelban/stats/issues/3120)), and the README now carries an FAQ entry for it. On macOS 27, Stats' fan helper fails to register (**#3716**). A third-party roundup reports that macOS 27 locked the SMC charge keys, so AlDente, batt and the `battery` CLI all charge past their limits ([macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/macos-27-battery-charge-limit-apps-broken-aldente-batt-charging-to-100-fix-2026/)). The same site reports that macOS 27 now draws the menu bar as one window, which broke Bartender, Ice, Hidden Bar and Barbee ([macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/macos-27-menu-bar-apps-broken-bartender-ice-icons-hidden-behind-notch-fix-2026/)). Both claims come from one secondary source and are not confirmed by Apple. The pattern is clear even so: **anything that writes to the SMC or manipulates other apps' icons gets broken every year**. Read-only, own-item apps do not.

Overhead is a quieter complaint. Stats' README admits that its Sensors and Bluetooth modules are the most inefficient, and that disabling them cuts CPU and power use "by up to 50%" ([README](https://github.com/exelban/stats)). Issue #3713 reports about 160 MB of memory at launch. Nobody publishes measured overhead for their own monitor.

## Battery displays now fail on honesty, not features

Apple's **macOS 26.4 Charge Limit** (an 80–100% slider in 5% steps) covers the basic "cap at 80%" need ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/16/mac-charge-limit-macos-tahoe-26-4/)). If macOS 27 has locked the SMC charge keys, it is also the only limiter left that works reliably. The pain has moved from enforcing a limit to understanding the charge state. An M4 Air owner asked whether the Mac at 80% bypasses the battery "or is it now just constantly cycling around 79-80%". The MagSafe LED stays amber whether the battery is charging or on hold ([MacRumors forum](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/80-charging-running-hotter-still-bypassing-battery.2480121/)). On macOS 27, AlDente's status "can still look normal" while the Mac overcharges, and issue #1817 reports contradictory status messages ([macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/macos-27-battery-charge-limit-apps-broken-aldente-batt-charging-to-100-fix-2026/)). Users also distrust Optimized Charging, which still charges to 100% ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/16/mac-charge-limit-macos-tahoe-26-4/)). And coconutBattery's charge figures disagree with macOS's ([Apple Communities](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255654391)). AlDente's own response to Apple's feature was to sell what Apple lacks: power analytics, health-over-time charts and heat protection ([AppHouseKitchen](https://apphousekitchen.com/we-got-sherlocked-or-did-we/)). Sparewatt's honest charge states, including Optimized Charging and Charge Limit, its power-flow view and its drain colouring answer this shift directly. The research found no quoted user demand for per-app drain attribution, so that need is inferred, not evidenced.

## Disk and file tools leave a "persistent, cheap, explained" gap

None of the major analyzers documents a persistent cache with incremental refresh. DaisyDisk, GrandPerspective and OmniDiskSweeper rescan on demand, and only niche tools cache results (diskr through FSEvents history, Mole by re-measuring every 6 h) ([docs.rs diskr](https://docs.rs/crate/diskr/latest); [Mole blog](https://mole.fit/blog/daisydisk-alternative.md)). **No surveyed source describes a time-budgeted scan.** Sparewatt's 15 s limit appears to be new. The louder complaint is space no scanner can see. Eclectic Light calls System Data "little more than a guesstimate" ([Eclectic Light](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/02/11/what-is-system-data-in-storage-settings/)). Users report System Data of 142 GB to more than 700 GB ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255156745)). Even DaisyDisk says purgeable space sits "outside of any scannable area" ([DaisyDisk](https://daisydiskapp.com/guide/purgeable-space)). Finder's "Calculate all sizes" is off by default because it slows the Mac, and its values go stale ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/how-to/view-folder-sizes-on-your-mac)). CleanMyMac's subscription and cancellation practices draw Trustpilot complaints ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.macpaw.com?page=7)).

File grabbing has a parallel gap. The native screenshot thumbnail can be dragged but disappears after a few seconds. Dropbox's popup broke the drag ("drag and drop doesn't work") ([Dropbox forum](https://www.dropboxforum.com/discussions/101001016/please-revert-the-new-screenshot-feature-on-macos-or-at-least-give-us-the-option/827159)). Single-purpose apps such as EasyShot exist only to keep that thumbnail on screen ([HN Showcase](https://hnshowcase.com/post/47436732/easyshot-macos-screenshot-thumbnails-that-don-t-disappear-af)). Dropover can open a shelf for each screenshot ([Dropover KB](https://dropoverapp.com/kb/screenshots)), but reviewers cite focus disruption ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/dropover-2/reviews)). No surveyed tool covers both screenshots and Downloads in one always-present spot, which is exactly what the Latest File card does.

## Prioritized gaps Sparewatt could exploit

| 5 | **Tahoe Menu Bar permission onboarding.** Detect when the item is hidden or disallowed and say so (Stats #3120) | Not covered as far as known |
| 6 | **History and sustained-threshold alerts** (Stats #1194, #2436, #3401, #3450 are open for years; iStat charges for this) | Not covered |

Two caveats apply. Reddit and App Store reviews could not be reached, so complaint frequency rests mainly on GitHub reactions and forums. And the macOS 27 breakage claims come from one secondary blog, not from Apple or primary issue pages.

## Conclusion

The competitive question has changed from "who shows the most metrics" to "whose numbers can be trusted after the next macOS and the next chip". Incumbents lose trust whenever a status goes stale, a sensor reads 0, or an item silently disappears, and the apps that write to hardware lose function as well. Sparewatt's read-only, single-item design is the structural advantage. The cheapest high-impact moves are to make honesty explicit (mismatch alarms, "unsupported" states, a reconciliation line for disk space, a visibility check for the Menu Bar permission) and then to add the history and debounced alerts that Stats users have requested for years without getting them.
